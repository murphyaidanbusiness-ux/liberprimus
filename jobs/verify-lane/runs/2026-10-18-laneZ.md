Reading: the attack detects the fresh line plant with a score far above the null max (+0.1254 against 0.0200). Stage 1,
however, recovered the clock phases only 46% right and a schedule that matches the true one under no global shift
([0, 9, 0, 17, 0] against [17, 20, 20, 11, 0]). Stage 2 then reached only noise-level quadgrams. The plant is not the cause
(the lane's own encipher reproduces it), and the call matches the lane's runner. The likely cause is stage 1's clock-path
recovery on this plaintext, which a single plant cannot separate from plant-to-plant variance. The numbers are identical
across reruns.

## What it means for the verdict

- NULL stands. The verdict rests on real LP2 scores against their nulls, and that comparison reproduces: no unit type's real
  score exceeds its null max (line 0.0185 against 0.0200, sentence 0.0203 against 0.0254, page 0.0195 against 0.0267,
  chapter 0.0241 against 0.0321), with z between -0.44 and +0.79.
- The detector still fires on a fresh line-dial plant (+0.1254 against 0.0200), so the null is not from a blind detector at
  line units.
- The decryption result changes what lane Z can say about decrypting line units. The lane's 4 of 6 is a six-plant figure,
  and one miss on one fresh plant is not evidence against it on its own; it does show stage 1 can detect a plant and still
  lock onto the wrong clock path. The verdict does not depend on decryption.

## Differences and what to trust

- Decrypted fraction, line units: the lane's six-plant figure (4 of 6) is the better-sampled number. Our single fresh plant
  (0 of 1) does not overturn it, but it shows the decrypt rate is not reliable on one plant. Likely cause: stage 1's clock
  path (46% phase accuracy), not the plant or the call wiring, which were both checked. Verdict unchanged.
