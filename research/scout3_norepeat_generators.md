# scout3_norepeat_generators — which generators natively give "no immediate repeat, otherwise flat"?

Scope: LP2 fingerprint = 86 adjacent identical runes in 12,901 within-page pairs (0.667%, vs 3.45%), IoC 1.000, increments flat, lag-2..7 coincidences normal, no period.
Evidence labels: [P] primary/verified here, [S] simulated here (code in `scratchpad/research/s3/*.py`, 300k-symbol runs, 29-symbol alphabets unless stated), [C] community consensus, [X] speculation.

## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

1. **No classical cipher, hand cipher or standard keystream generator has a native "output != previous output" rule.** I simulated or derived every system on the brief's list. Without exception they give 1/N doublets (3.4% for N=29) or *more*. The only things that produce an exact zero are (a) permutation-block streams and Markov "avoid-last" samplers, both excluded by LP2's lag statistics or by its non-zero residual, (b) an LCG/multiplicative orbit mod 29, which is periodic with period at most 28, and (c) an explicit feedback chain `c_i = c_{i-1} + s_i, s_i != 0`.
2. **The Uncovering Cicada wiki claim "low doublets traditionally point to autokey/autoclave" is wrong as a general statement.** On runic English, plaintext-autokey gives 4.8% doublets, lag-2 autokey 4.7%, ciphertext-autokey 2.7% (and that leaves a loud condIoC of 1.8), Trithemius 3.3%, difference-coding 4.0%, Gronsfeld 3.6% [S]. Only a *ciphertext-feedback* cipher whose plaintext increment can never be zero suppresses doublets, and that needs a flat-increment keying that is not standard autokey.
3. **Key theorem (derived, simple):** if the keystream is independent of the plaintext and its increments are flat, the ciphertext doublet rate is exactly 1/29 for *every* plaintext. So a plain PRNG/hash/LCG/RC4/MT keystream added to *any* plaintext cannot produce the 5x deficit. The key must be coupled to the ciphertext (feedback), or chosen adaptively (key-skip/rejection on the would-be output), or the plaintext itself has ~80% adjacent repeats (see §4). Corollary: brute-forcing "PRNG seed -> additive keystream -> English" is structurally the wrong test for the deficit. The orchestrator's chain/key-skip framing is the correct one; nothing in the literature improves on it.
4. **New exclusions from LP2 data computed here [S, on the transcription in `lp/lpdata.py`]:**
   - Doublets sit in-word and across word boundaries at about the same rate: within-word 63/10,028 = 0.63%, across-word 23/2,873 = 0.80%. Across-line 4/539 = 0.74%, across-sentence 1/164. So the rule is blind to layout. This kills "key/PRNG restarted per word" (would give ~99 across-word doublets and 0 within), and kills "per-word key shift + non-repeating base stream".
   - Doublet position mod B for B = 2..60 (global and per-page index): max z = 3.3 over 118 tests, i.e. nothing. Rules out block ciphers with distinct-output blocks of fixed size (Playfair-style, B=2: even/odd split is 51/35, p about 0.1, a Playfair would give 0/86 on one parity).
   - Doublets are uniform over the 29 rune values (chi2 = 26.0, df 28), no triples, shortest gap 6, gaps <=10: 4 observed vs 5.5 geometric expected. So they are memoryless events, not tied to a particular symbol (not an "F-interrupter" artefact).
   - Nonzero increment counts: chi2 = 41.8 on 27 df (p about 0.03, one test, not significant after look-elsewhere).
5. **The residual 0.66% is itself a clue.** An *exact* no-repeat rule gives 0%; a single-retry rule (redraw once if equal) gives 1/841 = 0.12% [S: 0.128%]. To get 0.66% you need the rule enforced about **83.7%** of the time with one retry (q = (1/29 - 0.0066)/(1/29 - 1/841); simulated 0.64%), or equivalently ~19% of would-be doublets surviving. Nothing natural in the generator literature does that. Three live explanations: a chain whose increment is zero with probability about 1/150 for a keyed reason; ~86 deliberate/glitch events layered on an exact generator; or stitching of about 2,500 independent segments of mean length 5.2 (86 x 29 joins), which are not word-aligned (above).

## 2. The "sampling without replacement / permutation stream" family (brief item 2)

- Concatenation of independent random permutations of 29: doublets only at block joins, rate (1/29)(1/29) = 0.119% analytically, 0.111% simulated. This is **not** 0.66%; getting 0.66% needs blocks of about 5.2 (joins at 1/5.2 x 1/29 = 0.66%), which would be a permutation-of-5-ish block structure; mod-B test (B=2..60) finds no block periodicity, and a window of length B>1 with distinct symbols would show lag-2 coincidences below 1 (here they are 0.98 +/- 0.05, 1.03-1.06 at lags 4-6; the pure model predicts 0.07-0.2 for B=29, and for B=5 about 0.75-0.85 at lags 2-4).
- RC4 on Z29 (S-box size 29, standard KSA/PRGA): 3.43% doublets, lag-2 1.00. Not suppressed. Fisher-Yates reshuffled each draw is iid, 1/29. Spritz-like likewise.
- Tetris-guideline "7-bag" and TGM "history" randomizers are the only well-known deployed generators with explicit anti-repeat logic (Tetris wiki; tetrisconcept.net). History-k gives lag-1..k deficits, so LP2's normal lag-2..7 rules out k>=2; k=1 reproduces the chain exactly.
- LCG/Lehmer mod 29: exact zero doublets only because the orbit never visits its fixed point; period at most 28, not LP2. LCG with a bigger modulus then reduced mod 29: ordinary 1/29.

## 3. "Differences" ciphers and Playfair (brief item 3)

- Running-difference encoding c_i = p_i - p_{i-1}: doublets iff p is an arithmetic progression of length 3: 4.0% on runic English [S]. Not suppressed, and the non-flat condIoC (1.97) would be glaring.
- A chain c_i = c_{i-1} + s_i **is** the inverse (running-sum) of a difference code, with s_i the flat nonzero keyed increment. That is the only structural way to turn "no doublets" into a design invariant: s_i = 0 is simply not in the output alphabet of the increment generator. Natural physical realisations: (i) a 28-symbol source alphabet masked by a unit u_i in GF(29)*, s_i = u_i p_i with p_i in 1..28 (F = index 0 excluded from the plaintext alphabet, which also explains why F is a special/"interrupter" rune in LP1); (ii) a Vigenere tableau with the identity (shift-0) row removed, keyed by previous ciphertext; (iii) a ciphertext-feedback alphabet shift (Chaocipher-like) where the plain symbol can't select "stay". Flag: all [X], none documented.
- Playfair: no doubled letters inside a digraph in the plaintext (X inserted) and the cipher cannot map a distinct pair to an equal pair; ct doublets within a digraph = 0, across boundaries about 1/N. Overall 1.66% for a 5x6 square with one null [S] (29 symbols + 1 null; 29 itself is prime so 29x1 is useless). It leaves a parity signature (all doublets on one parity), which LP2 lacks (51/35). Also the 30th "null" symbol would be visible as a 30th rune. Excluded.
- Nihilist: numeric addition, no suppression; Bifid/Trifid: fractionating, no suppression.

## 4. Plaintexts that would make a conventional cipher look like this [derived, S]

For a flat additive cipher with key increments never zero, or for Chaocipher-style progressive alphabets, ct doublet rate = (1 - f_pt)/28 or (1 - f_pt)/29 where f_pt is the fraction of adjacent plaintext repeats. 0.66% needs **f_pt about 0.81**. English f_pt is 2.5% [S: 2.465% on runic English]. So either the plaintext is a transform with ~5-fold run structure (unary/run-length/binary-ish), which the English-like word-length marginal argues against, or the mechanism is not "language plus additive key". I rate this low but it is the only route by which a *known* cipher (Chaocipher's Mellen property) natively suppresses doublets, so it is recorded.

## 7. Actionable leads (ranked by prior x testability)

1. **Direct-output test of "no-repeat generator" implementations with the 12,956 runes as the stream itself** (the layout is independent of content, so a generator emitting runes directly, then chopped into words by a separate process, is the Occam reading). Matrix: {Py2.7, Py3.2, modern Py} x {seed = int<2^32, str via hash(), str via sha512, bytes} x {`while x==prev: randrange(29)`, `choice([r for r in R if r!=prev])`, `randrange(28)` with skip-over, increment `randint(1,28)` mod 29, `randrange(1,29)` accumulate} x {MT, `/dev/urandom` not reproducible, OpenSSL/libgcrypt DRBG}. Test: first 24 runes (28^-24 false-positive). Expected ~1% prior, cost ~minutes per 2^32 on C. Note it must also explain the 86 non-zero doublets; if a hit appears the doublets are the diagnostic remainder.
2. **Treat the 86 doublets as a separate channel.** Under an exact generator they are events. Tests already done (rune value, layout, mod B, gaps) are null. Next: (i) delete one rune from each doublet and re-run the increment-IoC/condIoC (if the doublets are duplicated-glyph errors the cleaned stream must be exactly no-repeat; sanity check against the second transcription), (ii) check whether doublet positions are spaced by quantities from the book's own numbers (pages, line counts, page 56 gematria), (iii) correlate positions with image regions/marginalia on the actual page scans. Cheap, informs which mixture model (soft rule q about 0.84, zero-increment about 1/150, or glitch) is real.
3. **Soft key-skip with q about 0.84 / zero-increment rate about 1/150: find a mechanism that gives exactly that.** Candidate mechanisms to enumerate: increment = keyed byte reduced so that zero arises at one residue class of a 150-ish modulus; plaintext-dependent skip (skip applies only for certain plaintext symbols with total weight about 0.84); two-stage rule where the retry is itself unconstrained but the first draw excluded with prob 0.84. Fit via the exact doublet-gap/spacing distribution and the P(c_{i+1}=c_{i-1} | doublet at i) statistic (zero-increment chain predicts about 1/29).
4. **28-symbol plaintext with GF(29)* unit masking: s_i = u_i p_i (F = index 0 excluded from plaintext), key u_i from prime/gematria/hash streams, c_i = c_{i-1} + s_i.** Also the additive-offset version s_i = p_i + k_i with k_i rejected when it would make s_i = 0 (key-skip). Test on increments only (language-agnostic): increment IoC and the "u_i p_i" structure by checking whether increments mod unit subgroups (orders 2,4,7,14,28) are flat jointly with position classes. Overlap with the orchestrator's lane D ("prime-domain", "key-skip Vigenere") so first check what was already run; the new piece is the explicit *28-symbol-plaintext* assumption.
5. **Python-2.7 `hash(str)` and Python-3.2 sha512 word-seeded MT streams over the Cicada/English dictionary (about 3.6e5 words) used as the additive key or increment stream under the chain model.** Covers a gap in the "all 2^32 seeds" sweep (word seeds map to 64-bit hash values). Use the chain-increment test (flat nonzero increments + matching to the stream's increments, no plaintext needed): feasible because with c known and the increments s_i = c_i - c_{i-1} known, you test direct equality of s_i to candidate `randint(1,28)`-style sequences. This turns the key search into direct stream matching and makes it independent of English scoring.
6. **Stitched-segment hypothesis (segments of mean length about 5.2, not word aligned).** If a pad was assembled from many short exact-no-repeat runs, join positions carry P(equal) = 1/29; look for any second statistic that changes at joins (e.g., autocorrelation of increments mod 28 across a sliding window; Page-Hinkley change-point on increment distribution). Low prior, high rigor, and it would explain 0.66% without any soft rule.
7. **Low-prior literature candidates, ready to dismiss:** Solitaire, Mirdek, Card-Chameleon, Handycipher, LC4, Chaocipher, Enigma-like, RC4/Spritz, LCG/LFG, Playfair, permutation streams, Gronsfeld/Trithemius/autokey/difference: all simulated or derived above to give 1/N or the wrong sign. Do not spend compute on them for the doublet fingerprint. The only "known system" worth one cheap run is Chaocipher on a doublet-heavy plaintext (§4) to see whether a run-structured plaintext (unary/RLE) could give the same ciphertext profile; do it only if lead 2 shows the 86 doublets cluster in a way that suggests run-structure.
